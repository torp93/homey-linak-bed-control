LINAK Bed proxy

Flash the Bluetooth proxy for the Homey app, straight from your browser.

The Homey LINAK Bed app talks to your adjustable bed through a small ESP32 board placed near it. This page puts the firmware on that board for you. No ESPHome, no Python, no command line.

You need

  1. An ESP32 board — classic (WROOM), C3 or S3 all work.
  2. A USB cable that carries data, not just power.
  3. Chrome or Edge on a computer (Safari and Firefox cannot flash over USB).
  4. Your 2.4 GHz WiFi name and password. The ESP32 cannot join a 5 GHz network.
Your browser can't flash over USB. Open this page in Chrome or Edge on a computer. This page must be opened over https. Use the install link from the app's README.

Plug the board in, click Install, pick the serial port, then enter your WiFi when asked.

If the board is not listed, or flashing will not start

Some boards need to be put into download mode by hand the first time (classic ESP32-WROOM boards especially):

  1. Hold the BOOT button (sometimes labelled IO0 or IO9).
  2. While holding it, tap RESET (or, if there is no RESET button, plug the USB cable in).
  3. Release BOOT, then click Install and pick the port.

Boards with native USB are often detected straight away and skip this. Only reach for it if nothing shows up in the port list or the install stalls at the start.

After flashing, the installer asks for your WiFi over the same USB cable and the board joins your network. Then add a bed in the Homey app: pairing asks for the board's IP address, and the port is 6053.

Finding the IP: click Logs & Console here (the board prints IP Address: … when it connects), or look it up in your router's client list.
Tip: give the board a fixed IP. In your router, find linak-bed-proxy and set a DHCP reservation (sometimes called a static lease) for it. Otherwise its address can change and the Homey app loses the proxy until you update the address — the app's Repair screen on each bed lets you do that without re-pairing.

Placement

Put the board within a few metres of the bed, with as little concrete and metal in between as possible. The bed transmits weakly and its control box sits under the frame, so Bluetooth range is the most common source of trouble. One board can serve two beds.

The full guide, and the advanced configuration with WiFi and a fixed IP baked in, are in the setup documentation. This page is not affiliated with LINAK.
